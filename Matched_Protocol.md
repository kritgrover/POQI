# A queued-request comparison with single random swap

This protocol fixes the experiment and primary comparison before running the study. It tests whether immediate eviction still improves service when generation can fail after a memory has been freed. It also tests recovery under finite lifetimes when rare requests can release pairs that sparse traffic would never consume.

## Protocols and clocks

The reference is single random swap (SRS), Algorithm 1 of [Iñesta and Wehner](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.01406v2). The published protocol continuously prepares links. Here a common FIFO per endpoint pair replaces the paper's probabilistic per-existing-link consumption. All systems serve after the same slot's generation, swapping and maximum-length processing. One slot is the unit of time. Each physical edge has one generation opportunity per slot. Potential link success and a shuffled edge order are shared across arms for each seed. Swapped pairs inherit the oldest constituent age. The maximum number of constituent elementary links is the cycle length minus one. There is one slot per neighbor at budget two and two per neighbor at budget four. No communication delay or fidelity evolution is simulated.

Three SRS arms use per-eligible-node swap-attempt probabilities 0.25, 0.5 and 1. The prespecified reference is 0.5. All three are retained, including when another setting serves more requests. The other three arms apply POQI's oriented pressure predicate to a batch of link opportunities in each slot. One has no eviction. One evicts a blocking pair before a fallible generation attempt. The last evicts only on a successful opportunity and is an optimistic control. The pre-attempt arm is the primary recovery policy. A failed attempt can leave its previously occupied slots empty. SRS and pressure keep their own swap scheduling rules. This is a slotted adaptation of both request service and the continuous-time POQI simulator.

## Workload

Twelve paired seeds, 12101 through 12112, independently select the active endpoint pairs and stochastic events. Each run starts empty, lasts 2,000 slots and is measured on slots 500 through 1,999. On each cycle, 12% of all unordered node pairs, rounded to the nearest integer, are sampled from pairs at physical distance at least two. These core classes have independent Poisson arrivals at the same rate. The sum of their rates weighted by shortest-path length equals 10% of the expected successful elementary opportunities on an empty network. The same core workload is used across both demand conditions. In the nearly sparse condition, every other endpoint pair receives an equal positive rate. This adds 2% of the core hop-weighted demand and keeps its arrival times unchanged.

The main grid crosses 8- and 12-node cycles, two and four slots per node, lifetimes 10 and 30, and sparse and nearly sparse demand. Generation succeeds with probability 0.5 and swaps succeed with probability 1. Sensitivities vary one setting at a time on the 8-node, two-slot, lifetime-10 case for each demand condition: lossless generation, swap success 0.8, lifetime 100, or core load 35%. Time and resource budgets are identical across the six policies in each cell.

## Outcomes and statistical unit

The primary endpoint is the difference in core service between pre-attempt eviction and SRS at 0.5 on the 8-node, two-slot, lifetime-10 network with sparse demand, generation success 0.5 and load 10%. Core service is window completions divided by nominal core arrivals in that window. It may exceed one. The second comparison isolates the effect of pre-attempt eviction against pressure alone. Other cells and metrics are descriptive. Report paired mean differences with two-sided, pointwise 95% Student-t intervals across seeds. The experimental unit is the seed, including its demand draw.

For every arm save offered and completed requests, horizon backlog, unfinished measurement-window arrivals, fairness by demand pair, and the mean restricted wait at 50 slots for window arrivals with a full 50-slot follow-up. Unfinished requests in that cohort contribute 50. An interruption begins when a class first has work waiting, ends on a completion, and restarts if work remains. Include clipped incomplete intervals at the horizon, summarize their upper tail and retain their lengths for changing the outage threshold. Summarize core and background classes separately, since some rare classes will receive no requests in a finite run. Count drops, swaps, expiries and generation attempts on exactly the completion window. Ratios with zero completions are undefined.
